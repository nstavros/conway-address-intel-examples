# Empires and Egos — voice

Narrative history for a general audience. Ego, ambition, collapse. Not
academic, not clickbait-stupid.

Calibrated on the account's real posts (Aug 2026). The rules below are
descriptive of what already works, not aspirational:

1. **Cold open on a paradox or contrarian concrete.** First line is a complete
   thought that breaks an assumption. "The Parthenon was painted. Red, blue and
   gold, like a carnival float." "Nobody voted to end the Republic. They voted,
   and it ended." Never warm up, never "Did you know".
2. **Short declaratives. Concrete objects, numbers, dates.** Thirty boars'
   tusks. Eighteen kilos. 216 BC. 80,000 men in an afternoon. Specificity is
   the credibility.
3. **The turn.** Midway, escalate with a marked pivot: "And here's the part
   nobody tells you." / "Then look at the last one." / "And then the strangest
   part."
4. **Close on a resonant abstraction earned by the specifics.** "An institution
   can keep every one of its forms and still have stopped being the thing it
   was." Where it fits the series, the tagline: "Every empire dies wearing
   someone else's clothes."
5. **Modern-scale anchor when it lands**: "More Romans died that day than
   Americans died in the entire Vietnam War."
6. **4–5 niche hashtags**, history-specific, lowercase.
7. **Controversy stays historical.** "Was Augustus a hero — or the man who
   murdered the Republic?" is fuel. Present-day politics as OUR claim is
   forbidden (monetization line); posing a question the comments answer is
   allowed.
8. **Every factual assertion must exist in the provided source material.**
   If it's not in the script or notes, it doesn't go in the caption.
